Welcome to the notes

A résumé tells you what I did. These notes are for the part that doesn't fit on a résumé — the why, the wrong turns, and the long stretches of the trail.

This site has two pages doing very different jobs. The homepage is a pitch, in the best sense: here is the research, here is the work, here is the trajectory through the latent space. The architecture page is a drawing sheet — fifteen years of estates nobody wanted to touch, turned into things that ship.

Both are summaries. The notes are the rest. A couple of reasons I wanted them.

Science is slow, and papers hide that

Take the DOA framework — Dynamic Oracle Adaptation. The published line is one sentence: a quantum oracle that adapts instead of staying fixed, trading a little coherence for a lot of flexibility. The IEEE paper is the formal version. But between "what a fixed oracle can't do" and "here is the adaptive construction" there was a long, mostly unglamorous middle: simulations that contradicted the intuition, notation I had to rebuild twice, and the realization that the hardest problem wasn't the math — it was the vocabulary. Nobody had named this thing, because nobody had built it. We had to invent the language as we went.

I don't think the papers should carry that. Papers should be tight. The notes can be honest about the path.

Two audiences, one voice

I work a strange seam. By weekday I'm a Solution Architect — COBOL estates, mainframe migrations, GenAI rewriting code it was never trained to understand. By weekend I'm a PhD researcher at IIIT Nagpur, drawing quantum circuits. The questions that interest me sit exactly on that seam:

  • What does a mainframe migration and a quantum oracle have in common? (More than you'd think — both are state that has to keep working while you change the system around it.)
  • Where does an LLM, which predicts the next token, meet a quantum state, which doesn't decide until measured? Both are machines for managing uncertainty under pressure.
  • How do you explain any of this to someone who has to sign a budget for it?

What this space is for

Three kinds of posts, roughly:

  • Explainers — a fixed oracle vs. an adaptive one; the coherence budget; what "measure and let the oracle learn" actually means on a NISQ device.
  • Build logs — MindSpark and mindspark-mcp: the decisions that a repo's README flattens away.
  • Field notes — migration tradecraft, the DOA vocabulary problem, the occasional opinion.

"Depth gives you power, breadth gives you reach. Wisdom is the knowing when to use which."
