01 / About

The short version

I build systems that have to work — and lately, systems nobody has built before.

For fifteen years at Tata Consultancy Services and TRDDC, I've turned legacy enterprise estates into modern, cloud-native architectures: COBOL and mainframe workloads migrated to Java and .NET microservices, compliance turned into a service, and — most recently — GenAI put to work reading and rewriting code it was never trained to understand.

Two granted patents came out of that work, along with the habit of chasing a problem until it's genuinely solved, not just shipped.

Somewhere in there, the questions stopped being about migration and started being about computation itself. I'm now a PhD scholar at IIIT Nagpur, working at the edge of quantum computing, machine learning, and cryptography. My research centers on the DOA framework — Dynamic Oracle Adaptation — methods for making quantum oracles adapt rather than stay fixed. The first of these became the first quantum-computing paper published out of IIIT Nagpur.

I write production code on weekdays and quantum circuits on weekends. I like it that way.

02 / Research

Dynamic Oracle Adaptation

The DOA program asks a simple question with awkward consequences: what if a quantum oracle didn't have to be fixed in advance? Instead of hard-coding the black box a quantum algorithm queries, DOA methods let it adapt to the problem — trading a little coherence for a lot of flexibility.
